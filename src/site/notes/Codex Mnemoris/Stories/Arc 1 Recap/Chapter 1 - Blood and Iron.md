# Blood and Iron

_Session 01_

## Nyméra

The office is too hot and the numbers don't add up. I've checked the safe twice. Money is missing, not a lot, but enough to notice, enough to make my jaw tight. I put the papers down when I hear the argument start in the training room... I know the sound of it, that particular pitch where sparring tips into something uglier. I stay in the doorway for a moment and watch. Two fighters, hands and aggression, no weapons yet. I let it run. Sometimes they burn it out. Then I see the flash of steel and I'm already moving.

"Drop it." My fighter doesn't hear me, so I say it again and this time he does. I catch the visitor's wrist before he gets his hands on the blade, squeeze until it falls, and throw him toward the door. His friends are waiting outside. They give me the look that means they're considering their options. I give them nothing back until they run out of options and leave. I turn to my fighter. "Bring a blade here again and you don't fight for me." I pick up the knife, pocket it, and tell him to get his hands wrapped. I have errands. I step out onto the street and take the next tram I see coming.
## Bane

The ward I forgot to disable catches fire behind me and I'm already three stalls deep into the arcane market, moving like I'm here for the food. I'm not here for the food. A herbalist catches my wrist as I pass, gives me the look that says he knows exactly what I've done, and I give him the smile that used to get me free tea. He sighs and lets go. Good man.

An old flame peels out of an alley and presses a charm into my palm. Unstable, crackling, basically my type. I nod and keep moving. The ward is getting louder behind me so I toss the charm into it, watch the whole thing detonate into a cloud of sparkling light, and take a breath on a high balcony while the city spreads out below. A tram slides past underneath. I jump, hit the roof, skid, recover, and drop through the hatch like I planned the whole thing. I straighten my collar. A few passengers glance up. Nobody is impressed. That's fine.
## Freddie

The punch misses my face by a finger's width and cracks the wall behind me. I put my knife into the man's back before the sound finishes echoing. He goes down loud. His friend at the end of the alley shouts something about freaks and then turns and runs, which I find considerate. I pull a vial from my belt, pop the cork with my teeth, and drink. The mutagen hits fast. The city sharpens into something almost unbearable. I can smell the fear from here. I follow it.

He makes it three corners before I catch him. We go into a stack of crates and I come up with my hand at his throat, blade kissing skin, not cutting. "You're in luck," I tell him. "I think I was only supposed to get your friend." He struggles. I find the back of his skull and put him down clean. One casualty. Contracted target handled. I hear the pickup crew pulling in down the block and I'm already on the fire escape, timing the tram below. I land on the roof, crouch into the wind, and let the last of the mutagen burn off in the afternoon heat.
## Roa

The pantry is exactly where I thought it would be and the jar is exactly what I hoped. Rich people always have the best treats and they never finish them. I'm three bites in when I hear the floor creak. The maid is early. I move fast, back toward the air vent, nearly clean, nearly perfect, and then my dress catches on a screw and she sees me. She grabs a broom and throws it. Something comes out of my hands and the broom just stops existing. We both stare at where it was. Then I run.

I stop under a bridge when the shouting fades and sit with what happened. The magic surprised me. It keeps doing that. The bigger problem is that I can't go back to that pantry now, which means I've lost the best treat source in this part of the city. Freddie won't buy them for me. I don't know anyone else with access to that kind of kitchen. I wander for a while, not ready to go home, and when a tram hisses to a stop beside me I get on because the air inside is cool and I have nowhere else to be.
## Jax

I keep my breathing even while the boots thunder past. The guards don't see me pressed into the machinery. The explosion hit somewhere deep in the ship a few minutes ago and now the whole facility is chaos, scientists running, alarms screaming, guards locking down what they can. My first thought when it started was that something in the labs had gone wrong. My second thought was the girl. I move.

Finding her is easy. She's under the artificial willow, eyes wide, hands shaking. I grab her arm and we run. The first corridor is clear. The second isn't. A squad fans out ahead of us, weapons up, and I notice the hatch in the wall before I notice what my arm is doing. It rips the door clean off. I look at my hand for half a second, then throw the door at the guards and pull the girl through. Warm summer air hits us from outside, and below it is the bay. I don't think about what comes next. I grab her, I jump, and the thrusters I didn't know I had fire up and carry us out over the water. Behind us, The Water Lily burns. Reaching the shore I notice a tram stopped by a group of onlookers. I get in with the girl - Theta, and one stern look at the tram driver gets us pushing through the crowd into the city.

#### Impact

They hear surveillance drones buzz overhead. Are they from the floating island? Are they looking for the ribbet and the girl? Before they can investigate bigger problems arise.

The war rig comes from a side street without warning.

Freddie hears it first. A low vibration in the rails, wrong for the hour, wrong for the direction, and then the sound of an engine that has no business being that large on a city street. He is halfway to his feet when the rig connects with the side of the tram. The impact is not like a crash. It is more like the tram ceases to exist as a vehicle and becomes briefly a projectile. Glass fills the air in every direction, catching the last orange light of the evening and holding it for a fraction of a second, beautiful and wrong. Then the world goes sideways and dark.

Nobody on that tram chooses to lose consciousness. It simply happens.

#### The Ride

The first thing Nymera registers when she comes back is the smell. Oil, sweat, and the particular staleness of enclosed bodies in motion. Her wrists are bound behind her. She is on her side on a metal floor that shudders with the rhythm of a moving vehicle. Around her she can hear breathing, some of it ragged, and the occasional soft sound of someone trying not to cry. She counts the sounds without moving. She has been in bad situations before. The first rule of a bad situation is to understand it before you announce yourself.

She turns her head slowly. The truck bed is long and dark. Wooden slats line the walls. A canvas flap covers the rear. Through the gaps in the canvas she can see the city giving way to something with fewer lights. Sixteen, maybe seventeen people. Most of them still, some of them awake and frightened. Three of them she recognizes.

Freddie is two bodies to her left, already conscious, already assessing. His eyes meet hers. Nothing is said. Nothing needs to be.

The flying ribbit is further back, the girl from the tram still close to him, her small frame tucked against the wall. He is awake, jaw tight, watching the guards at the front of the truck bed with the flat attention of someone calculating odds.

Roa is near the canvas flap. She has her eyes open and is staring at the ceiling of the truck with an expression that is very carefully not panic.

Bane seems to be still unconscious. Or maybe he is faking it...

Nymera works her wrists against the rope quietly and methodically, the way she has learned to do everything important: without announcing it. The knot is good but not exceptional. Based on the noises and passing lights outside the truck has been going for a while already. Maybe there is still more time. She gets to work.

---

Freddie had freed himself within ten minutes of waking. He did not move afterward. He lay still and listened and mapped the truck the way he mapped every enclosed space: exits, weight distribution, number of threats, quality of light. The guards at the front were two, possibly three. Armed. They were not talking, which meant they were either professional or afraid of whoever had sent them, and professional and afraid often looked the same in the dark.

He had recognized the sound of the war rig before the impact. Not the specific vehicle, but the type. Junkjaws ran modified heavy rigs through the outer districts for raids, and had done so for years. He had not expected them this far into the city center. The fact that they had come this deep and this boldly told him something about the current state of the camp. He filed the information away.

Nymera caught his eye and he gave her the smallest nod. She understood it. They had spent enough years operating in parallel that their silences carried full sentences.

Across the truck, he watched Jax shift his weight almost imperceptibly. The ribbet was trying to loosen his own binding by leverage rather than manipulation, which would either work very quickly or not at all. Freddie noticed the girl beside him. He noticed the marks on the back of her neck before he could stop himself. He looked away.

---

Roa had freed herself by accident. She had been pulling at the rope trying to find the knot when her fingers found a loose end and the whole thing came apart in her hands, and then she was sitting in a moving truck with her wrists free and absolutely no idea what to do with that. She folded her hands behind her back and kept them there. The guard nearest to her had not moved.

She thought about the broom. She thought about the way the magic had come out without her choosing it, fast and total, and the look on the maid's face before she ran. She thought about what that same instinct might do in the back of a truck full of frightened people and decided, firmly, that she would keep her hands exactly where they were until someone who seemed to know what they were doing gave her a reason to use them.

The truck hit a pothole and the city sounds outside went thin and far away and were replaced by the sound of uneven ground and wind. They had left Atlantis proper. Roa watched the canvas flap and said nothing and waited.

---

Nobody saw Bane get on the truck. That was not a coincidence.

He had come to in the wreckage of the tram to find two Junkjaw crew members standing over him with the disinterested efficiency of people who had done this many times. They had his arms and were moving him toward the truck before he had fully processed what was happening, and in that half-second window he had made a decision. He went limp. He let them carry him. He listened to how many footsteps there were and where they were pointed and what the voices around him were saying. By the time they loaded him into the truck he had a reasonable picture of the immediate situation. He kept his eyes closed for another few minutes after that, just to be thorough.

When he finally opened them, he was in the back of the truck with the others. He looked at the canvas flap. He looked at the guards. He looked at the gap between two wooden slats near the floor, just wide enough to see through. Beyond it: open waste ground, the distant smear of city light already small, and the dark outline of an industrial complex growing steadily larger on the horizon. He was waiting like a loaded spring.

#### The Dawn of captivity

The truck stopped inside a fence line.

The doors opened onto noise, firelight, and the smell of machine oil and old blood. The Junkjaw camp occupied what had once been a factory district. The main building was a long concrete block with rusted gantries and broken-windowed upper floors. Surrounding it: warehouses in various states of use, vehicles in various states of modification, and people in various states of purpose. Fighters, mechanics, runners, and a particular category of person who was simply present, watching, with nothing useful to do and all night to do it.

The captives were pulled out of the truck and lined up in the yard. Guards moved along the line with the brisk assessment of people sorting livestock. Nymera stood straight and kept her expression level. Freddie stood behind her and studied the layout over her shoulder. Jax held the girl by the arm and said nothing. Roa stood at the end of the line and kept her hands together in front of her because it felt safer.

A guard stopped at Nymera. Looked at her for a moment too long. Said something to the man beside him in a low voice. The man beside him smiled. Then the first guard put his hand on her shoulder, casually, the way some men do when they want to remind you who is in charge of the space between you.

Nymera turned her head and looked at the hand. Then she looked at the guard. She did not move or speak. The guard held the position for about three seconds and then his hand came off her shoulder and he moved down the line. The man beside him stopped smiling. The first guard, desperate to gain his authority back, pulls back his huge fist to strike down the insolent prisoner. A quick bob and weave and the assailant founds himself staggering to keep his balance. The other guard puts an arm to his shoulder to stop him from making even bigger fool of himself. You heard a whispered word - "Mother". Fuck! They had recognized her.

In the commotion everyone's attention was turned on the violent guards. No one noticed Bane carefully stepping into darkness. When they noticed, he was already gone. Disappeared into the dying night. 

The sorting was brief. Fighters to the pits. Useful bodies to the labor lines. Anything that looked like money to the holding rooms. A runner appeared from the main building, spoke quietly to one of the senior guards, and pointed at Theta. Two guards separated her from Jax without asking. Jax moved forward. The nearest guard raised a hand, and from somewhere in the dark beyond the firelight there was the sound of a bolt being drawn on something large. Jax stopped. The girl looked back at him once before they took her through a door and the door closed.

Freddie put a hand briefly on Jax's arm. Not a reassurance. A reminder that the current moment was not the right moment.

---

The pit cell was ten meters wide and smelled of fear and old iron. A single bench ran the length of one wall. Five fighters were already inside when Nymera, Freddie, and Jax were pushed through the door, and two more were brought in behind them. The door closed with a sound that meant it.

A Katari fighter in the far corner looked up when they entered. He had been in the cell for eight days and it showed in his eyes but not in his posture. His name was Razhik. He said very little on first meeting but what he said was useful: the old man decides when a fight ends, the drug they offer before the fight is not worth what it costs you, and nobody has walked out of this place on goodwill alone. He said all of this quietly, without drama, and then went back to whatever he had been thinking about.

Nymera sat on the bench and looked at the door. Freddie walked the perimeter of the cell once, slowly, and then sat beside her. Jax stood in the center of the room for a while and then, finding nowhere better to be, sat down too.

---

The slave shacks were outside the main compound, separated from the factory by a chain-link fence with barbed wire along the top. Roa was pushed through a gate with eight others and found herself among twelve people who had been there long enough to understand that the collar meant something different from the rope. An iron band, bolted shut at the neck. She looked at it on the woman in front of her and felt the space around her own throat with one careful hand.

They fitted her with one before she could decide how she felt about that.

She sat in the dirt near the fence and thought about the space between the wire and the ground and whether it was wide enough and what she would do if she got through it and where exactly she thought she was going in the middle of an industrial wasteland with a metal collar around her neck. She had no answers. She kept the thoughts anyway, because having them felt better than not having them.

Across the yard, through two fences and the lit windows of a building she could not enter, she knew Freddie was somewhere. She held onto that with both hands.

---

Nobody saw Bane unless he wanted them to.

He found a high vantage point inside the factory before anything else. He needed to understand the shape of the place before he could be useful inside it, and being useful inside it was the only plan he had. From where he crouched on a catwalk above the main floor he could see two levels of operations happening at once: the public performance of the Junkjaw camp going about its evening, and the quieter infrastructure underneath it that told you who actually held power and where.

He found Roa through a gap in the fence. She was alive and sitting upright and appeared to be thinking hard about something. He found the cell block by process of elimination. He did not find Theta. He noted where he had not looked and filed that away for later.

Below him the camp started to wake up, fires burning lower and the light replaced by the sickly morning sun, voices risiing, the machinery of captivity grinding forward. Bane stayed where he was and watched and waited, which was the thing he had always been best at, even when he hated it.
