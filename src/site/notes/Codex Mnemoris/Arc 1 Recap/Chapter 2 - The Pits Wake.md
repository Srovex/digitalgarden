# Welcome to the Pits
*Sessions 01–05*

Morning in the Junkjaw camp arrived without ceremony. Light came through broken windows in long diagonal bars and lit up dust that had no intention of settling. The old Peepo Cola's factory floor was already loud before most of the city had finished sleeping. Bet collectors moved through the crowd checking slips. A smell of fried food came from somewhere near the east entrance and mixed badly with everything else. The place had the atmosphere of a festival run by people who did not care if you enjoyed yourself.

Up on the second level, Bane leaned against a pillar and watched it all from behind a black shawl he had found in an unattended pile of equipment the night before. He had slept three hours on the workshop floor, rejected the floor, returned to the dirty floor, and eventually stopped arguing with himself about it. He looked like he belonged here. He always looked like he belonged places. It was one of the few gifts that had never let him down.

He found his butterfly knife in a vendor's stall near the north wall. Sitting in a tray alongside other confiscated items from the tram, tagged and priced. He stood there for a moment looking at it with the specific feeling of a person confronting something they thought they had lost. He struck a deal with the vendor, a Giant with 3 eyes. He would give Bane the weapon if he manages to prank a mechanic named Ratch.

After a brief chat with Ratch he had to go and deal with a group of children who had stolen something from him. Bane looked at the vehicle Ratch had been working on, a heavily modified truck frame with more chassis than engine, thought for a moment, and spent the next few minutes doing something to its mechanics that he would later be unable to fully explain but that resulted in something that moved in all directions at once. Ratch returned to find his truck improved in ways he had not requested and looked at Bane for a long time without speaking.

The Giant gave Bane his knife and was so impressed that he gave him Freddie's broadsword as well.

He spent the rest of the day learning the architecture of the camp's social order. Every place like this had one: a visible hierarchy of guards and fighters, and underneath it a parallel structure of favors, information, and access that was where things actually happened. Bane was interested in the second structure. He had identified a guard named Hollis who seemed to hold an unusual amount of informal authority for someone without a senior rank, and he made his way toward him with the relaxed confidence of a man who was exactly where he was supposed to be.

By the time the main crowds started filtering in for the day's fights, Bane had told Hollis three stories, two of which were true, laughed at two of his, and been offered access to the second-floor viewing platform. He accepted as though it were a minor convenience.

---

In the pit cell, the morning had a different texture.

Razhik was awake before the light changed, sitting in the same position he had occupied the night before, apparently having decided at some point that sleep was a resource he could not afford to spend. Nymera had slept badly and woken with the particular clarity that comes from having no illusions left to protect. Freddie had not slept at all, which was not unusual for him and not something he mentioned.

Jax had talked in the night. Not sleep-talking but actual conversation, quiet and disjointed, filling the dark hours with fragments of himself that the daylight might not have allowed. He told them about his fold, about the smuggler's life in the Gutterspires, about the particular pleasure of a clean delivery and the way the bay smelled before a run. He told them about the girl, carefully, about the warmth that came off her that he could not explain and did not want to examine too closely. Freddie had listened to this last part with an expression Nymera could not read in the dark, and said nothing.

She had filed it away.

Outside Bane had found the pit cell by following the smell of the guards. He stood outside the correct door and had a brief argument with himself about the best way to announce his presence without alerting the guards. He resolved it by choosing volume over subtlety, shouting a sequence of creative insults through the wall specifically concerning Freddie's weapon, his fighting style, and several speculative assessments of his character.

Inside the cell, Freddie sat up from the bench.

Nymera looked at him. "That's Bane," she said.

"I know," Freddie said.

There was a pause.

"He recovered your sword," she added.

---

The cell doors opened late-afternoon without announcement. Guards stood in the corridor with the flat patience of people who did this regularly. Weapon selection was offered at a rack near the entrance to the pit: a range of blades and blunt instruments in varying states of maintenance. Freddie took a broadsword without hesitating. Nymera declined everything. Jax looked at the rack for a moment and then at his own hands and made his choice accordingly.

The sound hit them before the light did. The factory floor was full. Every level was packed, catwalks straining under the weight of bodies pressing toward the railing. Bet collectors moved through the noise with focused efficiency. The pit below had two-meter walls and a floor the color of old rust, and the crowd was arranged around it with the cheerful bloodlust of people who had paid to watch something get hurt.

---

From the second-floor platform, Bane saw Roa before she saw him.

She was serving food in the VIP section, a collar around her throat and an expression she was managing with visible effort. He watched her move between tables, her head slightly lower than usual, her hands careful. She set something down in front of a large orc lieutenant who did not acknowledge her, and then in front of an older man whose bearing suggested he was accustomed to being the most important person in any room he entered. He was called the Old Man Kardu. Also known as "Vest". Bane noted the older man and stored his face. He noted the orc and stored his face. He noted the collar and felt something shift in his chest that he converted, quickly and quietly, into a practical problem.

He began thinking about how collars worked.

Below, the pit fight started.

#### A Performance for the Crowd

The fight started well. The other team was capable, willing, and operating on the reasonable assumption that four captives from a tram ambush would be easier to break than four experienced fighters with nothing to lose. This assumption did not survive contact.

Jax hit with the full weight of arms that had been rebuilt for a purpose he was still discovering. Nyméra moved through the space with the specific economy of someone who does not waste energy on anything that is not the end of the problem. Freddie was where he needed to be, which was never the same place for long. Razhik absorbed punishment with the grim patience of a man who had learned to measure pain in units rather than reactions.

The crowd grew restless. A restless crowd told the people upstairs that adjustments were needed.

#### Red Cap

In the VIP section the Old Man Kardu grew restless and said something to his lieutenant. He quickly exited the room. Soon after a guard appeared at the pit wall and dropped small glass vials onto the floor of the fight. Small vials, glass catching the light. 

Nyméra picked one up and knew immediately what it was. R-Cap, or Red Cap as it was known on the streets. She had heard of it from fighters who came back from places like this changed in ways they could not quite name and did not survive long enough to fully describe. A performance enhancer, they called it.

She set the vial on the floor and put her foot through it. Looked at the others. No one reached for theirs.

Their opponents reached for everything available.

The shift was immediate and wrong in a way that was hard to name. The four across the pit moved differently after they took it. Not faster, exactly. More continuous, as though the normal small hesitations between actions had been removed. The fight changed. It became what the crowd had been waiting for: harder, faster, and significantly less certain.

When the fight ended, the crowd was satisfied. Some one in the crowd had recognized Nyméra. Maybe the Junkjaws had marketed the fight. The crowd loved her. They could her the shouts for "Mother" all the way back to the cooldown room.

---

Upstairs, in the VIP section, a runner appeared at Kardu's table and leaned down to say something in his ear. The old man's expression did not change. He nodded once and everyone around him was ushered out of the room.

Roa was moving with the others toward the exit when she caught movement in the floor near the baseboard, something small and quick. Saela, beside her, reached down and snapped it before Roa could see what it was, and they were outside in the corridor before either of them had time to discuss it.

Through the wall, Roa heard voices she did not recognise and a door closing with the sound of something important being decided. She filed it away with all the other things she did not yet have the context to understand.

---

Bane had been watching the collision between Hollis's growing suspicion and his own need to ask specific questions about collar removal mechanisms. He had not handled the balance well. Hollis was not stupid, and the questions about the collar were specific in a way that was difficult to explain casually. He pivoted, told a story about a woman he had once known in a similar situation, and watched Hollis decide whether to believe him. Hollis decided to, mostly, and Bane filed the experience away as a reminder that he was not as smooth as he thought he was when he actually needed something.

#### An offer you can't refuse

The cooldown room smelled of cold metal and fatigue. They had been there for a long time. Sounds of the crowds had already died down. 

The fight was over, but the blood on the pit floor was acting strange.

It pooled, which blood does. But then it kept moving, slowly, toward the center of the pit, gathering from the edges as though something beneath the floor was drawing it in. Freddie noticed it first and went very still in the way a person goes still when something in them recognizes what it is seeing and wants it badly and knows that wanting it is the problem.

Their confusion about the blood came to a sudden halt when the cooldown room door opened and guards started to stream in. Last one to enter was Kardu himself, which told Nymera something. He congratulated the fighters with the measured approval of a man who respected competence without being surprised by it, distributed payment, and then turned to Jax and asked, with the directness of someone who had stopped having time for preamble, what exactly he was.

Jax gave him a partial answer.

Kardu gave him a partial answer back. Bloom Index had offered a significant sum for Jax and the girl. Not money. R-Cap, in quantity. That was the detail that landed differently. Bloom was not paying in the currency of people who wanted something returned to them. They were paying in the currency of people who needed something to happen quickly. He then told them about the Berserker. The camp's champion had left. Not escaped, not removed. Left, in the night, through a wall, and the camp had a problem that needed solving before certain other problems became unsolvable.

The offer: find him. Bring him back or bring him down. In return, one prisoner freed per person, and a path out.

They looked at each other. It was not a long discussion.

#### Path to freedom

Bane and Roa met in the dark outside Warehouse 3 with a jar of fungril dissolver paste that Bane had found in a supply room and immediately understood the potential of. He opened it and dripped a single drop onto the ground to demonstrate. The concrete developed a perfectly circular hole. They looked at the hole for a moment.

"That should work," Roa said.

"Probably," Bane said.

Roa cast a light. It bloomed soft and steady between them, illuminating the collar and Bane's hands and both of their expressions, which were doing their best to project confidence. From Warehouse 3, through the wall, came shouts and a sudden bright flash under the door. Neither of them commented on it.

The first attempt grazed Roa's neck. She winced in pain and gritted her teeth. The second attempt cut deep. Blood started to flow more freely so she had to drink a potion, but she did not complain, which Bane noted. The guards noticed the light and started moving. The third attempt cost Roa dearly but still the collar held. Bane took a stabilizing sip from a small flask. Roa tilted her neck toward him again and said nothing. The fourth attempt produced a sound, a low metallic protest and then a snap, and the collar came free in Bane's hands.

He held it for a moment, looking at it. Then he put it on the ground.

Roa touched her own throat carefully, feeling the absence of it. Her had came back sticky with her own blood. She looked up at Bane with an expression that was not quite gratitude and not quite relief but was something in that territory, and neither of them said anything adequate, and then the guards arrived and there was no more time for anything.

Bane put Hollis's name in front of them and told a story about private recreation that was neither convincing nor entirely unconvincing, and in the end they were escorted back toward the main building with enough ambiguity about the situation to prevent anything worse from happening immediately.

#### Convergence

At the door of the main building, two groups passed each other.

Nymera, Freddie, and Jax were coming out with purpose and weapons and the particular forward lean of people who had been given a task. Roa and Bane were being brought in under escort with the particular upright dignity of people trying not to look like what they looked like. The two groups had perhaps four seconds of shared space in the doorway.

Freddie looked at Roa. She looked at him. His expression asked several questions at once. She shook her head slightly, which answered the most important one. Nymera noted the collar's absence and the redness at Roa's throat and stored both pieces of information without changing her expression. Jax glanced back once as he passed, not certain what he was seeing but filing the shape of it away.

Some quick thinking saves Roa and Bane from further questions. The hunters group demands their help in their mission to find the missing Berserker. The guards are easily intimidated by the champions of tonight's arena.

They are shown Berserkers quarters, barely a prison cell, with copious amounts of R-Cap on the floor. Freddie examines the broken door and gets a vision. Something massive and fast has gone trough here recently.

#### The Bull Fight

The trail was not difficult to follow if you knew how.

Freddie knew how. He moved ahead of the group through the industrial zone with the focused quiet of a man running a calculation, reading the ground the way he had been taught to read it. Disturbed gravel. A claw mark on a metal support beam at a height that required some thought. The pattern of what was missing from certain spots, vegetation trampled, light machinery knocked aside without effort. He shared what he found in brief sentences and kept moving.

The tracks led to an abandoned pub.

It was dark inside and the door was unlocked, which meant nothing good. Nymera went in first, Jax behind her, both of them moving quietly despite the evidence suggesting that quiet might not matter much. The first thing they saw was blood on the floor. Not pooled normally but gathered, moving slightly at the edges the way it had in the pit. The second thing was the sound, cutlery in a back room, something large shifting weight against a surface not built for it. The third thing was the silence that followed the sound, which was worse.

The blood near Jax's boot moved. He crouched and looked at it. Something inside it was circling, lazy and deliberate. He touched it with one finger, quickly, and pulled his hand back. The blood followed his fingertip for a fraction of a second before falling back.

From the back of the pub, a voice. Low and specific, without inflection.

"Have you come to bring me back? Kardu."

Not a question, exactly. More like a man reading the last line of a story he had written himself.

The Berserker came through the wall rather than the door. The distinction mattered. Where a wall had been there was an irregular opening and a great deal of displaced material and a figure that resolved itself into something larger than any of them had prepared for, even having been told. The impact caught Nymera and Jax both and sent them back. The blood on the floor rose in answer.

It did not rise as blood. The shapes it formed were low and quick and had too many angles and moved with the loose-limbed urgency of things that existed at the edge of what was possible. Hell hounds. The stories about the pit did not include this part, or if they did the stories were told by people who had not come back. Freddie and Bane stepped in from the doorway together and the fight became what it needed to be, brutal and close and without any space for anything except the immediate problem in front of each of them.

The Berserker went down after a long time and a great deal of effort. He went down like something that had been waiting to stop, which made it worse rather than better.

Freddie was given the task of ending it. He did not delegate and did not hesitate, which was the most honest thing about him.

Bane crouched over the Berserker's weapon when it was done. A maul, heavy and blood-dark, with something embedded in the head that caught the light in a way ordinary metal did not. He pried it loose and held it up. A gem, deep red, catching the firelight from the broken windows. He turned it once in his fingers and put it in his pocket without explaining himself.

#### At the Door

As they were preparing to leave they heard a voice. "Razhik," she said. "What is the meaning of this."

Not Junkjaw clother. The formation was too deliberate, the positions too tactical, the approach too quiet for camp thugs. They had moved through the darkness with the controlled register of people who hunt to live, and live to hunt. Nyméra looked at Freddie. Freddie looked at the door.

A group stepped into the torch-lit space inside the pub. They looked at the party. They looked at the body on the floor behind the party. They looked at the party again.

The woman at the front was a Katari. She looked at Razhik with an expression that moved through recognition and arrived at something more complicated.

Nobody moved for a breath. Everyone in the room was doing rapid calculations about what the other people in the room were likely to do next, and what the correct response to each scenario would be.

Whatever came next was going to require a conversation that nobody was fully equipped to have.
