# The Pride Arrives
*Session 06*
#### Earlier on the same day

While the fighters were bleeding in the pits and Bane was building an identity in the crowd, something else was happening further out in the industrial zone.

Liam and Zam were sitting by a small fire eating dry rations from a shared bag, having a conversation that was going nowhere in particular. This was not a problem. Eight days on a tracking mission with no clear endpoint teaches a group to make peace with conversations that go nowhere. It is better than the alternative, which is silence, and the silence that settles over a camp when people are beginning to quietly calculate the odds.

Zam was a faun, compact and precise, with the particular stillness of someone who had learned to be patient through actual practice rather than temperament. Liam was a simian, broad and warm, the kind of person a group unconsciously positions itself around when things get uncertain. They had been together long enough to fall into the rhythms of people who have stopped performing comfort for each other and simply have it.

Kiri appeared from the dark and took a ration bar without asking. She was their leader in the specific way that leaders emerge in small groups under pressure: not appointed, not announced, simply the person whose decisions the others find themselves following. She sat and looked at the fire and asked if Doran had come back yet.

He had not.

A few minutes later, something small moved through the undergrowth and resolved itself into a raccoon, which resolved itself into an elf, which sat down by the fire and began to report.

---
#### What Doran Found

He had been inside the camp for the better part of two days, moving as a small critter through spaces that would not accommodate a person. He had found Saela. He had found the children, all twelve of them, confined in Warehouse One, working tasks and getting beaten when the tasks were not completed quickly enough. He had found Razhik, who had been captured and was fighting in the pits.

He had also found a problem.

The camp was heavily guarded and operated with the internal logic of an organization that had been running for some time. There were too many guards for a direct assault. The children were not hidden, they were openly imprisoned, which meant any attempt to free them would be visible from every direction simultaneously. They would need a distraction significant enough to pull guard attention away from WH1, sustained long enough to move twelve children through a fence line and out into the industrial zone.

What they needed, in short, was more people.

Kiri listened to all of this with the focused attention of someone building a mental structure. Then she said: the beast that escaped the camp. She had heard about it from the outer edge of the zone something large, moving fast, leaving a particular kind of evidence. The kind of evidence a Berserker leaves.

They could not leave it loose in the civilian zone. And it might be an asset. A Berserker brought back to a gang that had lost it was leverage. Leverage was close enough to people.

They packed the fire and followed the tracks.

---

#### Two Groups, One Body

They heard the sounds of fighting before they saw the building.

Kiri signaled a halt and they listened from the dark: a fight concluding, the specific density of noise that accompanies multiple combatants in a small space, and then the particular silence that follows when the immediate problem has been solved.

They found the warehouse. They found the group of people standing over the Berserker's body, and Razhik standing with them, and Freddie holding a blade that had recently been used in a way that ended things.

Kiri looked at Razhik. Razhik looked at Kiri. The expression that passed between them covered a great deal of ground very quickly.

The introductions were brief. The parties sized each other up with the particular efficiency of people who have learned to read strangers fast, because strangers are frequently either useful or dangerous, and the sooner you determine which, the better. The party looked at the hunters from [[Codex Mnemoris/Factions/Low-Bridge Pride\|Low-Bridge Pride]]. The hunters looked at the party. Each group arrived at a provisional assessment that the other might be useful, or at least not immediately dangerous.

Freddie crouched beside the Berserker. The horn was thick, deeply curved, and had been part of the camp's reputation for as long as the camp had existed. He cut it free with the clean economy of someone performing a task that needs to be done rather than a statement that needs to be made. An uneven cut, but the horn came away. He stood and held it out toward Nyméra.

She took it without comment. Proof, trophy, or bargaining chip — the category could be determined later. It was evidence that something had been handled, and evidence had value.

Zam moved to Jax without asking permission and applied what she knew to the worst of his damage. The wounds closed cleanly. It was not nothing.

---

#### The Plan That Seemed Reasonable at the Time

The conversation happened in low voices with the fire of the city smeared against the horizon and the industrial zone spread dark around them.

The [[Codex Mnemoris/Factions/Low-Bridge Pride\|Low-Bridge Pride]] needed the children out. The party needed Theta and Jax out. These objectives were compatible with the correct distraction. What they had available as a distraction was: an established presence in the pit, a track record of crowd engagement, and the fact that Kardu had already expressed willingness to deal with them.

The plan that emerged was not elegant. It was the kind of plan that forms when multiple groups with different objectives and limited information try to identify what they agree on. They would cause a distraction large enough to pull eyes toward the main building. The Pride would use the window to move the children out through the eastern fence. The party would use the same window for Theta.

Razhik was quiet through most of this. He had been in the camp for nine days and he had certain views about plans formed by people who had been in the camp for less time, but he kept those views to himself on the grounds that the current situation had already exceeded his ability to predict it, and additional unpredictability might as well be pointed in a useful direction.

They were preparing to move when Doran looked up. Then Kiri looked up. Then most of the group looked up, because a sound had reached them from above - something massive, rotors in the dark, the particular presence of a large vehicle that was not bothering to conceal itself.

It passed overhead without slowing. The direction it was travelling was toward the camp.

No one said anything for a moment.

Then they moved.

---

#### What the Camp Had Become

The camp they returned to was not the camp they had left.

It was audible before it was visible: a different frequency of noise, lower and less rhythmic than the usual factory sounds, the specific register of a place that is generating violence rather than containing it. Fires were burning in at least two locations. The turbines that ran the camp's power were screaming at a load they had not been designed for. The guards at the outer perimeter were not positioned to keep people out - they were positioned to keep people away from specific internal locations.

Nyméra read this in three seconds and turned to Bane. He had read it already. A leadership transition was in progress, which meant the camp's chain of command was currently a contested document rather than an operating structure, which meant the rules that had governed their situation yesterday were being rewritten in real time.

This was both a problem and an opportunity, and which it was would depend entirely on how fast they moved and how well they read the spaces between.

---

#### Mirela

Warehouse Three was where the camp's civilian population had gathered. Not because anyone had told them to. WH3 was as far from the center of the current decision as the camp's geography allowed.

Hollis was there. He looked at Nyméra and Bane with an expression that suggested he had been expecting someone like them to appear at some point this evening, and he was not certain whether their arrival made things better or worse, and he had decided to remain observationally neutral until the question resolved itself.

Mirela Kord was there too.

She was a woman who carried her authority the way experienced people carry it. Not displayed, not asserted, simply present, the way gravity is present. She had been operating in and around the Junkjaw camp for long enough to know the difference between a situation that was resolving and one that was escalating, and she had placed this one correctly. She knew what was happening in the main building. She knew why.

She told them: Grak had been dealing with Bloom Index behind Kardu's back. Not a recent arrangement. Long enough that Bloom had provided real support: R-Cap in quantity, operational assistance, the kind of backing that allows a lieutenant to imagine himself as a principal. In exchange, Grak had offered something of value. Something that had arrived in the camp several days ago in the company of a ribbet who did not know how to make himself unnoticeable.

She did not say what the exchange rate was. She did not need to.

Nyméra told her they needed access to the main building. Mirela looked at her for a moment, measuring something. And she told Nyméra what she knew about the passages between WH3 and the main building's less monitored entry points.

---
#### What Kardu Knew

The main building was a fortress in the specific sense that it had been made into one very quickly by people who knew how to make things into fortresses: barricaded lower windows, guards at every entry point with the tense focus of people who understood that the fighting outside was coming inside regardless of what they did, and who intended to make it as expensive as possible.

Kardu was in the centre of it, and he was the calmest person in the building.

He looked at the party when they appeared and seemed to reach a rapid conclusion about how to file them. Not allies, not enemies, something more pragmatic: variables that could be influenced. He gave them information because information was currently a currency he could afford to spend.

Theta was in WH2. Grak had moved her there three days ago, when the Bloom Index arrangement became operational rather than theoretical. She was being held for transfer. The transfer was happening tonight. The transporter that had passed overhead on their way back was Bloom's - Lira Ventsyn, a Senior Biomantic Field Recovery Officer, had arrived on it with a small extraction team and a very clear objective.

Kardu had no interest in the girl. He had had no interest in her when she arrived, and her departure was not his concern. What was his concern was that his lieutenant had sold him out to a corporate entity for a drug supply, and that the resulting civil war was currently happening in the building directly adjacent to the one he was standing in.

He allowed them a short rest. They took it, and used it, and when they were done they were still in a bad situation but at least they were in it with their wounds dressed.

---

#### Bane Tries

The distance between the main building and WH2 was not large. Under normal circumstances it would have taken thirty seconds.

The circumstances were not normal.

Grak's men had positioned themselves between the main building and WH2 with the specific goal of controlling that corridor, because they understood that anyone trying to reach Theta would need to cross it. Bane looked at the corridor, looked at the guard positions, and made the kind of rapid assessment that is approximately correct and approximately too optimistic.

He moved into the corridor and was spotted almost immediately. The guards opened fire without significant hesitation, which told him several things at once about how the orders in this part of the camp were currently structured. He got back to cover with his skin intact, which was a better outcome than it could have been.

The corridor was watched.

---

#### What Roa Did

Roa had been watching the corridor too, but she had been watching it the way she watched most things that presented as problems - with the particular quality of attention that was looking for the edges rather than the centre.

She closed her eyes for a moment. When she opened them, something was running.

It looked like Theta. It had Theta's height, Theta's gait, the particular way Theta moved when she was frightened, the small hunch of the shoulders, the too-fast steps. It ran away from WH2, across the open ground between the buildings, in a direction that was plausible and wrong.

The guards saw it.

There was a moment - a genuine moment of uncertainty, the kind that happens when people trained to react quickly encounter something their training did not cover and then two of them moved after it. The shout that went up brought another three from their positions.

It was not a perfect illusion. It would not hold for long. Roa knew this when she made it. She also knew that she didn't need perfect. She needed a window, and the window was now.

Kardu's men registered the disturbance with the opportunistic speed of fighters who had been waiting for exactly this kind of moment. The corridor that had been controlled became chaotic. The chaos made a shape, and the shape had a path, and the party ran through the path.

---

#### What Was in the Dark

The blood hounds were back.

Bane saw them first, in the gap between WH3 and the route to WH2. Low shapes moving through the fighting with the angular wrongness he had seen in the pub, except here they were moving through people rather than attacking specific targets, a kind of ambient violence that the regular fighting could not account for and did not know how to respond to. Guards who had been fighting Kardu's men an instant before turned to deal with something else entirely, and the ground was briefly more complex than any of them could track simultaneously.

The party ran. They quickly cleared the melee and were running back to Mirela Kord at Warehouse 3. They cleared the last corner and pulled to a full stop.

On the other side was a Bloom Index operative in full tactical kit and four Corporate Security guards, and all five of them were blocking their way.

They pointed at Jax. "Come with us peacefully, Specimen 3488"

Everything stopped for the second time that night.
